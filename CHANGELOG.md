Deterministisch aus der Übungs-ID, an den beiden Ladegrenzen, nur Text bewegt sich; ein Test hält jede Position in [20 %, 47 %]. `StableHash` ersetzt `string.GetHashCode()` auch bei Wortbausteinen und Match-Spalte.

OpenAI-kompatibel: keine `temperature` an gpt-5-Modelle (400), `Retry-After` über 20 s wird gemeldet statt abgewartet, `finish_reason: length` wird benannt, leere Antworten schalten `json_schema` nicht mehr ab, die letzte Ablehnung steht in der Meldung. `Friendly()` an allen vier Stellen, an denen der Lerner eine Tutor-Meldung sieht.
- **Planer: Lesen/Hören vor den Drills, mit eigenem Anteil.** Der Input-Block kam nach den Fokus-Drills, die bis unter 20 s Rest laufen – ein 20-Minuten-Plan für einen neuen Lerner hatte gar keinen Lesetext. Jetzt 35 % des Budgets vor den Drills: 20 Minuten = ein Text und zwei Diktate. Die Schreibaufgaben im Prüfungsformat (Teil 1, 600 s) fielen unter der flachen 420-s-Grenze bei jedem Budget heraus; ab 20 Minuten dürfen sie den Slot nehmen.
- **Oberfläche.** Dunkelmodus: dunkler Kontrasttext auf den hellen Akzentfarben (7,58:1 statt 2,32:1), hell die orange Sekundärfarbe (5,04:1 statt 3,0:1). Die OpenAI-kompatiblen Tutoren teilen jetzt einen `SocketsHttpHandler` (Verbindungen leben 5 Minuten), beide Tutoren sind `IDisposable`, und die Registry entsorgt den ersetzten Tutor. „Alle Daten löschen“ vergaß die `ReadinessSnapshots` – nach einem Reset stand der alte Trend wieder auf Heute; der Isolationstest zählt jetzt jede Konto-Tabelle aus dem EF-Modell statt aus einer Hand-Liste. Ein Hörtext mit zwei Personen wurde von einer Stimme gelesen, samt der Namen („Lena: …“) – „wer sagt was?“ war so nicht zu hören. Hörtexte werden jetzt in Sprecherzüge zerlegt (Inline-Form „Lena: … – Markus: …“ der Inhaltsdateien und Zeilenform des Generators), jede Person bekommt ihre Stimme (bekannte Namen und Anreden fest, Fremde abwechselnd – Piper thorsten/kerstin, im Browser nach Stimmennamen), der Name selbst wird nicht gesprochen; ein Inhaltstest verlangt das von jedem als Gespräch angekündigten Text. - **Kein fremder Name mehr im Kurs** (`NameFallback`). Leere Namensfelder fielen auf den Namen des Autors zurück – in einem öffentlichen Repository das falsche Vorgabeverhalten: Ein Fremder wurde von seinem eigenen Kurs als jemand anderes begrüßt. Der Platzhalter kommt jetzt aus einem kleinen Vorrat, gezogen über einen stabilen Hash der Konto-Kennung (FNV-1a, nicht `GetHashCode` – das ist pro Prozess zufällig und hätte denselben Lerner nach jedem Neustart umbenannt). Ein Konto sieht damit immer denselben Namen, zwei Konten selten denselben. Der Vorrat ist bewusst männlich: 19 Stellen im Inhalt sagen „Herr {Nachname}", und ein weiterer Vorrat verlangt zuerst variable Anreden.
- **Drei Dateien trugen einen absoluten Pfad vom Rechner des Autors** und damit dessen Windows-Kontonamen ins öffentliche Repository (ein Prompt-Dokument, die beiden Harness-Skripte); ein Kommentar in `install-piper.ps1` nannte zusätzlich die 8.3-Kurzform. Die Werkzeuge nehmen ihren Pfad jetzt aus der eigenen Skriptposition oder aus einem Argument, und ein Test durchsucht den ganzen Quellbaum, damit keiner zurückkommt. Der Name in `LICENSE` bleibt – dort gehört er hin.

- **Ein Zeitlimit, das zum Anbieter passt** (`TutorPreset.DefaultTimeoutSeconds`, Feld „Zeitlimit je Antwort" auf Einstellungen). `Draft()` baute die `TutorSettings` bisher ohne `Effort` und `TimeoutSeconds`, sodass jedes Speichern beide still auf die Record-Vorgabe zurückdrehte und kein Limit über 120 s zu halten war. Gemessen auf einem lokalen 7B (Ollama, GPU): 6,7 Tokens/s, eine ganze Bewertung sind ~790 Prompt- plus ~800 Antwort-Tokens, also 117 s – die alte Grenze schnitt sie drei Sekunden vor dem Ende ab, und der Lerner sah nach zwei Minuten Warten „Zeitüberschreitung". Lokale Presets (Ollama, LM Studio, eigener Server) starten deshalb bei 300 s, gehostete bleiben bei 120 s; der Wert ist pro Konto einstellbar (20–900 s) und wandert beim Anbieterwechsel mit.
- Nebenbefund, der in die Ollama-Beschreibung gewandert ist: Ollamas Standard-Kontext von 4096 reicht für eine ganze Bewertung nicht – derselbe Prompt lief damit 600 s ohne jede Antwort und mit `num_ctx 8192` in 117 s durch. Das JSON-Schema kostet dabei fast nichts (122 s mit, 139 s ohne).

- **Englisch als zweite Erklärsprache** (`HelperLanguage`, migration `AddHelperLanguage`, set on Einstellungen). The interface stays German - that is the language being learned. What switches is the bridge: the 924 vocabulary prompts and 75 translation sentences (for those two types the prompt IS the helper language), the second column of every grammar table, the contrastive notes on 35 exercises and 23 nodes, and the 16 lesson explanations that named Serbian. 1,153 strings in total, plus 16 rewritten explanations.
- The contrastive notes are authored, not translated. "sa + instrumental → mit + Dativ" is useless to someone whose first language has no cases; the English note contrasts with English instead ("English prepositions have no case at all"). Where Serbian and German happen to agree, the English note says the opposite - the same rule, a different learner.
- One rule per field, and they differ on purpose: a prompt falls back to Serbian if no English one exists, because a vocabulary card without a prompt has no question at all; a note does NOT fall back, because showing a Serbian note to someone who chose English is worse than showing none. Content tests make the first fallback unreachable in practice and would fail the build if a Serbian string ever lost its counterpart.
- The swap happens in one place, `NameTemplate.Render(Exercise, LearnerView)`, next to the name substitution - the same boundary where shared catalogue content becomes one learner's copy. That matters more than it looks: speech is rendered from the same text and cached by its hash, so a prompt swapped anywhere else would be shown in one language and spoken in the other.
- Tests: 256 (was 253). Rendering, both fallback rules, five content guards, an E2E flow that switches the language and checks a lesson and the Themen chip, and screenshots of a lesson in English at 1280×800 and 375×812, light and dark.

- **Zielniveau B2 oder C1** (`LearnerProfile.TargetLevel`, migration `AddTargetLevel`), set on Einstellungen next to the exam date, each choice with its consequence written out. It moves exactly one thing: the band cap in `SessionPlanner.RankFocusNodes`. A C1 learner reaches `WS.C1_GEHOBEN` and the B2.2 grammar in the focus slot; a B2 learner never sees a C1 node there. `Ability.TargetDifficulty` is untouched, because aiming higher must not mean being handed items far above measured ability, and the placement is unchanged - it calibrates the B1/B2 boundary either way.
- Heute shows a second line "C1-Nähe" for C1 learners (`LearnerAnalysis.C1Proximity`: weighted mastery of the C1 and B2.2 nodes, flagged as an estimate while the data is thin). Deliberately not a second readiness number - there is no C1 exam blueprint here, and `Prüfung B2` now says so outright when the target is C1. The app bar and the Themen intro follow the setting; the shell learns about a save through a small scoped `LearnerProfileState`, so the label moves without a reload.
- Tests: planner tests for both directions (a C1 target reaches above B2, a B2 target never does), an E2E flow that switches the level and checks the shell and the exam page, and a screenshot test that renders the touched pages at 1280×800 and 375×812 in light and dark.
- **Branche im Profil** (`Occupation`: IT, Pflege, Bau, Medizin, Büro, Handel, or unset; migration `AddOccupationField`). The effect is deliberately small and explainable: matching vocabulary nodes get +0.15 in the focus ranking (Pflege/Medizin → `WS.GESUNDHEIT_KOERPER`, Bau → Wohnen/Arbeit, IT → `WS.IT_SOFTWARE`, …), and among otherwise equally suitable exercises the ones tagged for that field win the tie. It is the last word before chance and never overrides a real weakness - a nurse weak on the passive still gets the passive first, and a test pins that down. When it decided, the reason says so: "Vorgezogen, weil du in der Pflege arbeitest." Unset behaves bit for bit like before, which is what keeps the existing planner tests honest.
- **Der Kurs spricht dich mit deinem Namen an.** The content used to greet its author: 82 occurrences of "Aleksandar" / "Micić" across 23 files are now the tokens `{Vorname}`, `{Nachname}` and `{Name}`, and `LearnerProfile.FirstName` / `LastName` (migration `AddLearnerName`, the old unused `Name` column renamed) fill them in. Empty fields fall back to the author's own name, so nothing about his experience changes. Rendering happens at the boundary where content stops being shared - `LearningService` for the session's exercises and the answer result, `Lektion` for the story - never in the catalogue, which is a singleton several accounts read at once. `Exercise.Answers` is deliberately left untouched (the checker compares against it verbatim), and two tests hold the line: no content file may contain the literal name again, and no answer may contain a token.
- **Motivation ohne Punkte.** Fortschritt gains three cards read straight from the FSRS state: *Wörter, die sitzen* (memory stability past 21 days), *Fällig in den nächsten 7 Tagen* (a seven-bar forecast, with everything overdue counted on today so a backlog cannot hide), and *Behalten* (mean current recall probability over the scheduled items). Heute gains a weekly goal in minutes (`WeeklyGoalMinutes`, default 60, migration `AddWeeklyGoal`) and, on Mondays, a two-line Wochenrückblick about the week that ended - attempts, accuracy, items that stick, the hardest node. All deterministic; no tutor, no XP, no leaderboard (`KONZEPT.md` §5 now says why).
- One deviation, deliberate: the card says "davon diese Woche wiederholt", not "neu diese Woche". No history of past stabilities is stored, so the moment an item crossed the three-week line cannot be known, and a precise-looking guess would be worse than a duller true number.
- **Bereitschaft mit Richtung.** A new table `ReadinessSnapshots` keeps one reading per learner and day, written when the dashboard is opened and no row exists for today (migration `AddReadinessSnapshots`; a unique index on learner+day makes two tabs racing harmless). Heute then shows "▲ 3 % seit letzter Woche" next to the number, and nothing at all until a snapshot from six to eight days ago exists. The window is a range on purpose: nobody opens the app on a fixed schedule, and demanding exactly seven days would show a trend only to someone who never misses one.
- **Klartext, wo der Lotse etwas vorschlägt.** All 26 grammar nodes gained a `plainTitle` ("Hauptsatz: Verb an Position 2 & TeKaMoLo" → "Satzbau: das Verb steht an zweiter Stelle"). Heute's session suggestion, the weak-area list and the Monday review use it; Themen and Fortschritt keep the precise term, because that is where a topic is looked up rather than assigned. A content test requires every grammar node to have one and refuses a copy of the title.
- **Kleinigkeiten mit Wirkung.** Starting or resuming a session took one to three seconds with no sign the click had landed - the buttons now disable themselves and show a spinner. The placement stops asking the harder item of a topic once the easy one was missed: the sibling step is marked done with score 0 and the reason "übersprungen – Grundlage fehlt", which is what Stefan meant by "kein echtes Überspringen" (the daily-loop E2E now expects 2 of 28 after one wrong answer, not 1). Fortschritt folds mastered nodes into a "Sitzt (n)" panel so the page is about what still needs work.
- **Musterlösungen halten, was die Rubrik verlangt.** A content test checks every rubric line that names a detectable structure (Konjunktiv II, Passiv/Passiversatz, Relativsatz, temporal clauses, Plusquamperfekt, comparison structures) against the model answer's text. 13 model answers failed it and were rewritten - a rubric demanding a structure the model does not show teaches that the structure was optional.
- The old free-text `Occupation` (tutor prompt context) keeps its content and moves to `JobTitle`; the scaffolded migration would have dropped it, so it copies the text across before the column changes type. The course story stays as it is - it is the author's story, IT-flavoured by design; the setting steers drills and vocabulary, not the lessons.
- **German that sounds German.** Listening tasks are now rendered by [Piper](https://github.com/rhasspy/piper) (MIT) with the voice `de_DE-thorsten-medium` (CC0) instead of the browser's Windows voices, which are the older concatenative Hedda/Katja/Stefan and audibly robotic. Piper runs as a short-lived child process on this machine - no API key, no network call, nothing sent to a third party - and renders a sentence in well under a second. Set up once with `tools/install-piper.ps1` (~140 MB into `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Lotse`, nothing in the repo); where it is not installed, `ITextToSpeech.Available` is false and the browser voices are used exactly as before, so CI and a fresh clone are unaffected.
- **A voice per character.** `de_DE-kerstin-low` speaks the women of the story, `de_DE-thorsten` everyone else, chosen from the speaker label of the line (`SpeakerVoices`), which is why "Sabine (im Stand-up)" and "Herr Krüger (Mail)" resolve as well as the plain names. Unrecognised speakers - a new character, or an institution like "IT-Hotline" - keep the default voice rather than being guessed at from a name. Reading a lesson aloud and the per-line Vorlesen button both use it; without the second voice installed, every role speaks with the default one.
- Rendered audio is cached as WAV under the data directory, keyed by a hash of voice + speed + text, and served from `GET /api/tts/{key}.wav`. Deliberately an endpoint rather than a static folder: the fallback authorization policy covers endpoints, so a spoken tutor reply stays behind the login, and the key is validated as one of our own hashes before it is combined into a path.
- `playAudio()` reports three outcomes rather than a boolean. "stopped" is the one that matters: treating a deliberate interruption as a failure would have made the browser voice start talking again right after the learner pressed stop. Stopping also resolves the pending promise, so an interrupted playback can never leave `SpeakAsync` awaiting forever.
- Startup logs which voice is active (`Lokale Sprachausgabe aktiv: ...`) instead of leaving it to be discovered on the first Vorlesen.
- Tests: 198 (Core 129, Web 69). The Piper tests render for real where it is installed - umlauts and `ć` included, which is what proves stdin is written as UTF-8 - and skip loudly with `Assert.SkipWhen` where it is not, which is also what keeps CI on Ubuntu green.
- Docs: setup step in README, `docs/UPUTSTVO.md` (1c) and `docs/GITHUB.md` (what stays out of the repo, what to run after a fresh clone).

## 0.8.0 – 2026-09-04

The release shaped by a test with ten simulated learners (`docs/NUTZERTEST_2026-09.md`, average 7.0/10): every finding
below names the personas that hit it.

- **FSRS-5 spaced repetition** (`Fsrs`, `ReviewScheduler`). Each item carries a memory stability and a difficulty; it comes due when its recall probability falls to 90 %, a hard-won recall of a nearly-forgotten item grows stability most, a lapse shrinks it without throwing it away. Published default weights, no training; SM-2 states convert on their next review (migration `AddFsrsMemoryModel`). Retrievability is available per item (`ReviewState.RetrievabilityAt`).
- **Rule-based text analyzer without a tutor key** (`FreeTextAnalyzer`) - the gap eight of ten personas named first. Perfect with haben/sein, verb position after subordinators and fronted adverbials, the comma before dass/weil, umlaut and ß spelling, case after prepositions (gender from the vocabulary bank), n-declension, missing articles, verb + preposition, formal register, the greeting formula, length, coherence; false friends as hints. Every finding carries a catalogue code and lands in the journal and the learner model. Chosen for precision: a clean B2 text produces nothing.
- **Sessions are idempotent** (Dragan, Milica, Stefan): starting resumes an open session of the same kind instead of stacking a second one; finishing a placement closes every other open placement. "Beenden" asks - pause, finish, keep going (Tamara); a paused placement resumes from the dashboard, and daily sessions run beside an open placement (Jelena).
- **Quick placement** - 14 items, about eight minutes (Jelena, Nikola, Tamara).
